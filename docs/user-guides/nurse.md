## Getting Started

### Account Setup

1. **Receive Account Information**
   - Your facility administrator will create your account
   - You'll receive an email with login instructions

2. **First Login**
   - Go to MetaPharm Connect login page
   - Enter your email and temporary password
   - You'll be prompted to set a new password

3. **Two-Factor Authentication (MFA)**
   - MetaPharm requires MFA for all healthcare professionals
   - Set up authentication in your account settings
   - Use Google Authenticator or similar app

4. **Verify Professional Credentials**
   - Your nursing license and credentials will be verified
   - Process typically takes 24-48 hours
   - You'll receive confirmation email

### First Steps

1. **Update Your Profile**
   - Click Profile icon (top right) → "My Profile"
   - Add your photo and specialty
   - Set your work schedule and shift times
   - Enable notifications for your work hours

2. **Select Your Facility**
   - If you work at multiple facilities, select your primary one
   - You can switch facilities in Settings → Work Locations

3. **Review Assigned Patients**
   - Your facility admin will assign you a patient list
   - You can view all your patients in the Dashboard

## Medication Ordering Workflow

This section explains the complete medication ordering process from creation to delivery, including all validation steps and best practices.

### Complete Medication Ordering Process

**Step 1: Initiate New Order**
1. Click "New Medication Order" from Dashboard or Medication Orders widget
2. Patient selection options:
   - Click "Select Patient" to open search dialog
   - Search by patient name, ID, or date of birth
   - Results show in real-time with patient details
3. Select the correct patient from results
4. System confirms patient allergies display below patient name
5. Click "Continue" to proceed to medication selection

**Step 2: Select Medications with Historical Context**
The system helps you select appropriate medications with clinical context:

1. **Search for Medication**
   - Type medication name (e.g., "Aspirin")
   - Or search by NDC code
   - Or use "Browse Medications" to filter by category
   - Results show available dosages and quantities

2. **Review Medication Details**
   - Medication name and strength
   - Available dosages
   - Current inventory level
   - Patient's medication history (if previously used)
   - Typical dosing suggestions
   - Contraindications warnings

3. **Select Dosage and Quantity**
   - Choose strength from dropdown (100mg, 200mg, etc.)
   - Enter quantity needed
   - System shows:
     - Maximum quantity allowed per order
     - Typical quantity for patient's frequency
     - Minimum and maximum per dose

4. **Set Urgency Level**
   - **Routine**: Standard 24-48 hour delivery
   - **Urgent**: Expedited (same day if before 2pm)
   - **Emergency**: Call pharmacy directly, order for immediate pickup
   - Urgent orders flagged for faster pharmacist review

5. **Add Medication Notes**
   - Click "Add Notes" for special instructions
   - Examples:
     - "Patient has difficulty swallowing tablets"
     - "Administer with food"
     - "Check renal function before administration"
     - "Patient requested liquid formulation"
   - These notes visible to pharmacist and delivery personnel

6. **Add Medication to Order**
   - Click "Add to Order"
   - Medication appears in order items list
   - Continue adding more medications, or proceed to review

7. **Repeat for Additional Medications**
   - Add multiple medications to single order
   - System prevents duplicate medications in same order
   - If patient needs same med twice daily, add with note

**Step 3: Review Order Before Submission**
1. **Verify Correct Patient**
   - Confirm patient name and ID displayed
   - Double-check against patient record if uncertain

2. **Review All Medications**
   - Each medication listed with:
     - Dosage strength
     - Quantity
     - Urgency level
     - Notes (if any)
   - Remove medications using "X" button if needed
   - Edit medication using "Edit" button if needed

3. **Check Against Allergies**
   - System shows patient's known allergies
   - Red flag if any medication conflicts with allergies
   - Contact pharmacist if allergy concern exists
   - Do NOT proceed if unresolved allergy conflict

4. **Add Order-Level Notes**
   - Click "Add Notes to Pharmacist" if needed
   - Examples:
     - "Patient in pain, high priority"
     - "Pt requested preferred pharmacy"
     - "Please confirm delivery window with patient"
   - Character limit: 500 characters

5. **Verify Patient Contact Information**
   - Confirm delivery address
   - Confirm phone number for delivery contact
   - Confirm preferred delivery time window if applicable

6. **Submit Order**
   - Click "Submit Order" button
   - System performs final validation:
     - Insurance coverage check
     - Interaction check
     - Prescription validity check
   - You receive confirmation with:
     - Order number (reference: ORD-XXXXX)
     - Timestamp
     - Estimated delivery date/time
     - Patient and pharmacist notification status

**Step 4: Post-Order Actions**
1. **Order Confirmation**
   - You receive in-app notification
   - Patient receives notification (method per preferences)
   - Pharmacist receives order for processing

2. **Track Order Status**
   - Order appears in "My Orders" dashboard
   - Status progresses through workflow:
     - **Pending**: Awaiting pharmacist review (typically < 30 min)
     - **Under Review**: Pharmacist checking interactions and coverage
     - **Approved**: Ready for dispensing
     - **Dispensing**: Pharmacist preparing medications
     - **Dispensed**: Medications prepared, packaged, labeled
     - **Ready for Delivery**: Awaiting delivery personnel pickup
     - **In Transit**: On delivery route
     - **Delivered**: Successfully delivered to patient
     - **Cancelled**: Order cancelled (with reason notification)

3. **Address Approval Issues**
   - If order marked "Needs Approval", check notification message
   - Common issues:
     - Insurance coverage question
     - Medication not in stock (alternatives suggested)
     - Interaction warning requiring clarification
   - Click "View Details" in notification
   - Message pharmacist if approval unclear
   - Pharmacist may contact you for clarification

### Ordering Medications with Historical Context

The system learns from patient medication history:

**Using Medication History**
1. When selecting medications, system suggests:
   - Medications patient has previously used successfully
   - Typical dosages for this patient
   - Medication frequency patient takes
2. Click "Use Previous Dosage" to auto-fill common doses
3. Adjust if patient situation differs

**Common Order Scenarios**
- **Routine Refill**: Patient medication running low, order same dosage
- **Dose Change**: Doctor changed dosage, adjust quantity accordingly
- **New Medication**: Doctor prescribed new medication, add to new order
- **Urgent Request**: Patient needs medication urgently, mark urgent

### Managing Order History and References

**View Past Orders**
1. Click "My Orders" in Navigation
2. View list of all orders you've placed
3. Filter by:
   - **Patient name**: Click filter, select from list
   - **Date range**: Select start and end date
   - **Status**: Show only delivered, pending, cancelled, etc.
   - **Facility**: If working at multiple locations
   - **Medication**: Search for specific drug
4. Click on order to view full details including:
   - Original order date/time
   - Items ordered
   - Notes entered
   - Pharmacy who fulfilled

   - Patient signature/confirmation (if applicable)

**Duplicate Recent Order**
1. Open past order
2. Click "Reorder"
3. System creates new order with same medications
4. Adjust quantities if needed
5. Add new notes if situation changed
6. Submit as new order

**Export Order for Records**
1. Open order details
2. Click "Export as PDF"
3. Document includes:
   - Order number and date
   - Patient information
   - Medications and dosages
   - Delivery confirmation
   - Your name and credential
4. Use for facility records or patient files

### Ordering Restrictions and Approvals

**Understanding Ordering Restrictions**

Your facility may have specific ordering restrictions:

**Controlled Substances**
- Some medications require special approval
- System flags these automatically
- Pharmacist review required
- May require supervisor approval from your facility
- Takes longer to process (plan ahead)

**Quantity Limits**
- Certain medications have maximum per-order limits
- System shows limit when selecting medication
- Example: "Max 30 tablets per order"
- Pharmacist can approve exceptions if medically justified
- Message pharmacist if need exceeds limit

**Insurance & Coverage**
- System checks insurance coverage in real-time
- If medication not covered:
  - Notification shows "Coverage issue"
  - Alternative medications suggested
  - Shows patient cost difference (if applicable)
- Pharmacist confirms coverage before dispensing
- Patient may need to approve cost if not covered

**Prescription Validity**
- Orders checked against patient's prescriptions
- System confirms:
  - Doctor's prescription exists for medication
  - Prescription is still valid (not expired)
  - Dosage matches prescription (or notes variance)
- Flagged if no prescription found (pharmacist confirms)

**Facility-Specific Rules**
- Your facility may block certain medications
- Examples: Due to allergies common in care population, cost controls
- System prevents ordering blocked medications
- Contact facility manager if need to override

### Medication Order Workflows by Urgency

**Routine Orders (24-48 hour delivery)**
- Standard process described above
- Pharmacist reviews during regular hours
- Typical turnaround: 4-6 hours for dispensing
- Delivery scheduled within 24-48 hours

**Urgent Orders (same day)**
- Mark as "Urgent" in Step 2
- System prioritizes for immediate review
- Typical turnaround: 30 minutes for approval, 1 hour for dispensing
- Delivery typically same day if ordered before 2pm
- Higher success rate if ordered before noon
- Contact pharmacy if extremely time-sensitive

**Emergency Orders (immediate need)**
- For truly urgent medical situations
- Use phone to call pharmacy directly
- Create order in system afterward for documentation
- Do not rely on system for true emergencies
- Emergency contact number: [listed in facility procedures]

### Best Practices for Medication Ordering

**Before Ordering**
1. Verify patient identity (check name and DOB)
2. Confirm medication needed (check prescription or care plan)
3. Check patient allergies (system shows, but review)
4. Confirm no recent delivery (avoid duplicate orders)
5. Verify patient delivery address current

**When Ordering**
1. Be specific with notes (help pharmacist and delivery person)
2. Double-check quantities (catching errors before submission)
3. Use urgency appropriately (routine only if patient can wait)
4. Group related medications (easier for pharmacy and patient)
5. Don't over-order (avoids medication waste)

**After Ordering**
1. Confirm order number received
2. Check status updates regularly
3. Message pharmacist if approval delayed
4. Prepare patient for delivery arrival
5. Document order in patient record per facility policy

## Pharmacy Patient Records Access

This feature allows nurses to access patient medication and pharmacy records maintained by MetaPharm Connect, providing clinical context for medication orders and patient care.

### Understanding Pharmacy Patient Records

The pharmacy maintains comprehensive patient records including:
- **Medication History**: All medications ordered through MetaPharm
- **Dispensing Records**: What was actually dispensed and when
- **Delivery Information**: Delivery confirmations and patient receipts
- **Interaction History**: Past allergic reactions, adverse events
- **Insurance Details**: Coverage information and billing
- **Patient Preferences**: Preferred pharmacies, delivery methods
- **Clinical Interactions**: Drug-drug interactions checked by system
- **Patient Consent**: Records of what patient authorized

**Why Nurses Need Access**:
- Verify patient's current medications from pharmacy perspective
- Check what was delivered vs. what was ordered
- See delivery confirmations and receipt dates
- Monitor adverse reactions reported by patient
- Understand insurance coverage issues
- Plan medication orders based on delivery history

### Accessing Patient Pharmacy Records

**Method 1: From Patient Record**
1. Open patient record (see Patient Search and Selection)
2. Click "Pharmacy Records" tab
3. Patient's pharmacy records load in sidebar
4. View all sections below

**Method 2: From Medication Order**
1. When creating medication order, click "View Pharmacy Record"
2. Opens pharmacy records for patient alongside order form
3. Compare recent orders and current medications
4. Make informed decisions on new medications

**Method 3: Direct Access**
1. Click "Patients" in Navigation
2. Select patient
3. Click "Pharmacy Records" button
4. Full pharmacy record loads

### Pharmacy Patient Record Sections

**1. Medication Profile**
Shows all medications in patient's pharmacy profile:
- Medication name and strength
- Quantity currently dispensed
- Frequency of use
- Date medication was added to profile
- "Active" or "Discontinued" status
- Notes about medication

**2. Order History**
Complete list of all medication orders:
- Order number and date
- Medications included
- Quantities ordered
- Order status (pending, dispensed, delivered, cancelled)
- Pharmacy that filled order
- Your name (ordering nurse) on order
- Delivery confirmation status
- Patient's signature/confirmation (if required)

**Filter by**:
- Date range
- Status (all, delivered, cancelled, pending)
- Medication name
- Order type (routine, urgent)

**3. Delivery History**
Tracking of all medication deliveries:
- Delivery date and time
- Delivery address
- Delivery personnel name
- Delivery status (on-time, late, refused)
- Patient confirmation/signature
- Special handling notes (fragile, cold chain, etc.)
- Proof of delivery (photo or signature)

**4. Dispensing Records**
Pharmacy's record of what was actually dispensed:
- Dispensing date and time
- Medications dispensed with exact quantities
- Dosages and formulations
- Pharmacy location that filled order
- Pharmacist who prepared (name)
- QC check confirmation
- Expiration dates of dispensed items

**5. Interaction and Allergy Profile**
System-maintained clinical safety records:
- Documented allergies (color-coded by severity)
- Previous adverse reactions to medications
- Documented drug interactions detected
- Foods or supplements patient avoids
- Medical conditions affecting medications
- Last updated date
- Who reported each allergy/interaction

**6. Insurance Information**
Coverage and billing details:
- Current insurance carrier
- Plan type and coverage level
- Patient's copay or coinsurance
- Medications covered vs. not covered
- Prior authorization requirements
- Insurance effective dates
- Billing address on file

**7. Patient Preferences**
Recorded preferences for deliveries and communication:
- Preferred pharmacy location(s)
- Preferred delivery address(es)
- Delivery time window preferences
- Special delivery instructions
- Preferred contact method
- Language preferences
- Communication frequency preferences

**8. Clinical Notes from Pharmacy**
Pharmacist notes and consultations:
- Drug therapy management notes
- Consultations with patient
- Clarifications with doctor
- Alerts and recommendations
- Follow-up required items
- Date and pharmacist name for each note

### Reviewing Medication History Before Ordering

**Check Before Creating New Orders**
1. Open patient's pharmacy records
2. Review "Medication Profile" section
3. Check "Order History" for recent orders
4. Verify delivery confirmations (patient received medications)
5. Review "Interaction and Allergy Profile" for:
   - Known allergies to medications
   - Previous adverse reactions
   - Drug interactions to watch for
6. Review "Insurance Information" for:
   - Coverage of medication you want to order
   - Prior authorization needed
   - Patient's copay responsibility
7. Create informed order using this information

### Common Pharmacy Record Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Patient Says They Never Received Medication**
1. Open patient pharmacy records
2. Click "Delivery History"
3. Find the delivery in question
4. Check delivery status and date
5. Contact pharmacy with delivery number
6. May require redelivery or medication replacement

**Scenario 2: Patient Asks About Medication Interactions**
1. Open pharmacy records
2. Review "Interaction and Allergy Profile"
3. Check "Clinical Notes" for relevant pharmacist consultations
4. If unclear, message pharmacist with specific question
5. Share relevant information with patient

**Scenario 3: Insurance Won't Cover Medication**
1. Open patient pharmacy records
2. Check "Insurance Information" section
3. See which medications are/aren't covered
4. Note prior authorization requirements
5. Contact pharmacist before ordering if coverage unclear
6. Pharmacist may suggest alternatives with better coverage

**Scenario 4: Patient Having Adverse Reaction**
1. Open patient pharmacy records
2. Click "Interaction and Allergy Profile"
3. Check previous reactions documented
4. Add new reaction using Adverse Reaction Reporting (see dedicated section)
5. Verify pharmacy is notified of new reaction
6. Confirm patient record updated with allergy

### Exporting Pharmacy Records

**For Documentation**
1. Open patient pharmacy records
2. Click "Export as PDF"
3. Options:
   - Full record (all sections)
   - Specific section (e.g., medication history only)
   - Date range (last month, last 3 months, etc.)

## Medication Administration Recording

This critical feature documents when medications are administered to patients, creating an accurate record for compliance, audit trails, and continuity of care.

### Why Document Medication Administration

**Legal and Compliance Requirements**
- Required by healthcare regulations (HIPAA, Swiss data protection laws)
- Part of patient's permanent medical record
- Audit trail for medication verification
- Defense against medication errors or misadministration claims

**Clinical Requirements**
- Track medication adherence and timing
- Identify any missed or refused doses
- Monitor patient response to medications
- Enable accurate reporting to doctors

**Quality and Safety**
- Verify dosage and route documented
- Track adverse reactions in context of administration
- Identify patterns (e.g., always refuses at certain times)
- Support future medication planning

### Understanding the Medication Schedule

Before administering medications, understand the patient's schedule:

**View Patient's Medication Schedule**
1. Open patient record
2. Click "Medication Schedule" tab
3. View complete information:
   - All active medications with names and strengths
   - Prescribed dosages
   - Scheduled times (e.g., 8am, 2pm, 8pm)
   - Frequency (once daily, twice daily, as needed, etc.)
   - Route (oral, IV, injection, inhalation, etc.)
   - Special instructions (with food, before bed, etc.)
   - Last documented administration (date, time, person)
   - Next scheduled administrations (upcoming times)
   - Medications marked as "PRN" (as needed) and conditions for use

**Understanding Medication Timing**
- **Scheduled**: Fixed times (breakfast, lunch, dinner, bedtime)
- **Interval-based**: Every 4 hours, every 6 hours, etc.
- **PRN (As Needed)**: Given when patient needs it
  - Pain medications (when patient in pain)
  - Anxiety medications (when patient anxious)
  - Anti-nausea (when patient feels nauseous)
- **One-time orders**: Given once, then discontinued

### Logging Medication Administration

**Step 1: Prepare for Administration**
1. Verify medication order (prescription)
2. Check medication name, strength, and expiration
3. Verify patient identity (two-factor check: name + DOB)
4. Check for allergies
5. Explain to patient what you're administering
6. Verify patient consent (or per facility policy)

**Step 2: Document Administration in System**
1. Open patient record
2. Click "Medication Administration" tab
3. Click "Log Administration" button
4. System shows medication selection form

**Step 3: Select Medication**
1. **Medication Selection**
   - Click dropdown to see all medications
   - System shows:
     - Medication name and strength
     - Prescribed dosage
     - Last administration time
     - Current administration time due
   - Select the medication administered
   - Click to confirm selection

**Step 4: Enter Administration Details**
1. **Dosage Administered**
   - Pre-filled with prescribed dosage
   - Edit if different amount given
   - Common reasons for variation:
     - Patient took only half dose
     - Medication unavailable, given alternative strength
     - Patient refused full dose
   - Always note discrepancies in comments

2. **Route of Administration**
   - Select from dropdown:
     - **Oral (PO)**: By mouth (tablets, liquid, capsules)
     - **Intravenous (IV)**: Into vein (injection, infusion)
     - **Intramuscular (IM)**: Into muscle (injection)
     - **Subcutaneous (SC)**: Under skin (injection)
     - **Inhalation**: Inhaled (nebulizer, inhaler, spray)
     - **Rectal (PR)**: Rectal suppository
     - **Topical**: Applied to skin (cream, ointment)
     - **Transdermal**: Patch applied to skin
     - **Sublingual (SL)**: Under tongue
     - **Other**: Specify in notes
   - System confirms route matches prescription

3. **Time Administered**
   - Pre-filled with current time
   - Edit if administering late dose
   - Must be documented as close to actual time as possible
   - Do NOT backdate medications (affects audit trail)
   - If medication given earlier in day:
     - Note actual time given
     - Contact supervisor if significant delay

4. **Site (if applicable)**
   - For injections, specify injection site:
     - Left arm (upper, lower, forearm)
     - Right arm (upper, lower, forearm)
     - Left leg (thigh, calf)
     - Right leg (thigh, calf)
     - Abdomen (left, right, center)
     - Other (specify in notes)
   - For IV: specify IV line location
   - For topical: specify area of body
   - Rotation of sites important for some medications

5. **Lot/Batch Number**
   - System asks for medication lot number
   - Found on medication container
   - Important for:
     - Traceability (recalls, quality issues)
     - Expiration date verification
     - Product tracking
   - Enter lot number in field

6. **Administration Notes** (Optional but Recommended)
   - Click "Add Notes" to document:
     - Patient condition at time of administration
     - Any difficulty administering (patient refusal, difficulty swallowing)
     - Patient reaction or response
     - Special circumstances (administered with food, etc.)

## Adverse Reaction Reporting

This critical feature documents any negative reactions or side effects patients experience from medications, protecting patient safety and updating medical records.

### Understanding Adverse Reactions

**Types of Adverse Reactions**
- **Allergic Reactions**: Immediate or delayed immune response
  - Examples: Rash, hives, swelling, itching, difficulty breathing
- **Side Effects**: Expected but unwanted effects of medication
  - Examples: Nausea, drowsiness, dizziness, headache
- **Adverse Drug Reactions (ADR)**: Unexpected serious effects
  - Examples: Organ damage, severe bleeding, severe allergic response
- **Drug Interactions**: Problems from combining medications
  - Examples: Increased side effects, reduced effectiveness
- **Idiosyncratic Reactions**: Unusual reactions specific to individual
  - Examples: Patient unusually sensitive to medication

**Why Report Immediately**
- Updates patient's allergy/reaction profile
- Prevents future doses of same medication
- Alerts pharmacist and doctor to pattern
- Helps identify medication interactions
- Protects patient from harm
- Creates legal documentation of event

### Reporting an Adverse Reaction

**Step 1: Recognize and Stabilize Patient**
1. Stop medication administration immediately
2. Assess patient's condition
3. Provide supportive care as needed
4. Call doctor if serious reaction
5. Do not ignore or minimize patient concerns

**Step 2: Access Adverse Reaction Report**
1. Open patient record
2. Click "Adverse Reactions" or "Safety Alerts" tab
3. Click "Report New Reaction" button
4. System opens reaction reporting form

**Step 3: Select the Medication**
1. **Medication Name**
   - Click "Select Medication" dropdown
   - Search by medication name
   - System shows:
     - Medication name and strength
     - Date patient started medication
     - Dosage patient was taking
     - List of medications patient currently taking
   - Select the medication that caused reaction

2. **When Did Reaction Start**
   - Select date and time reaction occurred
   - Include:
     - Exact time if known
     - Timeframe if exact time unknown (e.g., "within 30 minutes of dose")
   - Important for determining cause (immediate vs. delayed)

3. **Relationship to Medication**
   - System asks: "How certain is this medication the cause?"
   - Options:
     - Definite (reaction clearly linked to medication)
     - Probable (reaction likely from medication)
     - Possible (medication could be cause, other factors involved)
     - Unlikely (medication probably not the cause)
   - This helps pharmacist assess probability

**Step 4: Describe the Reaction**

1. **Type of Reaction**
   - Select primary reaction type:
     - Allergic reaction
     - Side effect
     - Severe adverse reaction
     - Gastrointestinal (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea)
     - Cardiovascular (chest pain, palpitations, hypertension)
     - Neurological (dizziness, confusion, seizure)
     - Dermatological (rash, hives, bruising)
     - Respiratory (difficulty breathing, cough, wheeze)
     - Other (specify)
   - Can select multiple types if multiple symptoms

2. **Severity Level**
   - **Mild**: Uncomfortable but not dangerous
     - Examples: Mild rash, mild nausea, mild itching
     - No immediate intervention needed
   - **Moderate**: Significant and requires treatment
     - Examples: Severe rash, vomiting, significant dizziness
     - May require medication or intervention
   - **Severe**: Life-threatening or requires emergency care
     - Examples: Anaphylaxis, severe breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness
     - Requires immediate medical intervention
   - **Death**: Reaction resulted in patient death
     - Requires immediate reporting and serious investigation

3. **Detailed Description**
   - Click "Add Description" field
   - Write detailed account including:
     - **Onset**: When reaction started (immediately, 5 min, 1 hour, etc.)
     - **Symptoms**: All symptoms observed
       - "Patient developed rash on arms and legs within 10 minutes"
       - "Patient complained of severe itching on face and neck"
       - "Patient's lips began swelling"
     - **Progression**: Did reaction get worse, stable, or improve?
     - **Patient's reported experience**: What did patient say?
     - **Your observations**: What did you observe?
   - Be specific and factual
   - Avoid assumptions ("probably anaphylaxis" vs. "severe swelling and difficulty breathing")
   - Include context if relevant:
     - Was medication taken with food? On empty stomach?
     - Was medication new for patient?
     - Had patient taken medication before without reaction?

4. **Patient's Response to Reaction**
   - What symptoms did patient experience?
   - What interventions did you perform?
   - Did reaction improve, worsen, or stay same?
   - What medications or treatments given for reaction?

**Step 5: Actions Taken**

1. **Immediate Actions**
   - Check all actions you performed:
     - Stopped medication administration
     - Called doctor immediately
     - Called ambulance/emergency services
     - Provided antihistamine
     - Provided epinephrine
     - Provided antiemetic (anti-nausea)
     - Monitored vital signs
     - Elevated patient's legs
     - Other (specify)

2. **Medical Interventions**
   - What treatments were given?
   - Who administered treatments?
   - When were treatments given?
   - What was patient's response?

3. **Follow-up Plan**
   - Will medication be restarted? When?
   - Alternative medication selected? Which one?
   - Follow-up appointments scheduled?
   - Doctor orders for monitoring?
   - Patient counseling provided?

**Step 6: Medication History Context**
1. **When was first dose?**
   - Date medication started
   - First reaction occurrence
   - Multiple reactions to same medication?

2. **Has patient had similar reactions before?**
   - To this medication previously?
   - To similar medications (same drug class)?
   - To other medications?
   - System shows patient's allergy profile for comparison

3. **Other medications being taken**
   - System shows all current medications
   - Helps identify drug interactions
   - Helps identify if reaction is pattern
   - Helps identify if new combination caused issue

**Step 7: Notify Healthcare Team**
1. System automatically notifies:
   - Pharmacy (to add allergy to profile)
   - Patient's doctor (for clinical decision-making)
   - Your facility manager (for serious reactions)
2. You can optionally:
   - Add message to pharmacist or doctor
   - Request immediate callback
   - Flag as "urgent" for serious reactions

**Step 8: Submit Report**
1. Review all entered information
2. Verify accuracy and completeness
3. Click "Submit Adverse Reaction Report"
4. Report is submitted and cannot be edited
5. Receive confirmation with report number
6. Report appears in patient's safety profile

### Handover Practices and Standards

**Quality Handover Notes**

- **Focused**: Highlight what next shift must know
- **Timely**: Complete before shift ends

**What NOT to Include**
- Negative comments about patient or family
- Assumptions ("Patient probably didn't take medication")
- Unprofessional language
- Personal opinions ("I think patient is just complaining")
- Rumors or unverified information
- Complaints about previous nursing care

**When to Add Urgent Alerts**
- Mark as "URGENT" if:
  - Patient safety concern
  - Significant change in condition
  - Doctor communication needed
  - Medication issue
  - Potential harm risk
- Use system alert function to flag next shift
- Consider calling next shift nurse if very urgent

### Documentation Best Practices

**Timing**
- Complete handover notes before end of shift
- Don't rush - take time to be thorough
- Include information from entire shift
- Don't backdate notes

**Language**
- Use professional medical terminology when appropriate
- Define terms (don't assume reader knows abbreviations)
- Use patient-respectful language
- Be specific (not "patient doing OK" but "pain 2/10, eating well, alert")

**Completeness**
- Include all shifts you worked (if extended shift)
- Note what you didn't do (important if medication due)
- Document refusals or non-compliance
- Include patient communication context when relevant

**Accuracy**
- Only document what you personally observed
- Include timestamps when important
- Distinguish between facts and observations
- Correct errors (per facility policy)
- Never alter previous notes

## FAQ & Support

### Common Questions

**Q: Can I order medications for patients not in my assignment?**
A: No, you can only order for your assigned patients. Contact your facility admin to add patients to your list.

**Q: How quickly are medication orders typically processed?**
A: Most orders within 2 hours during business hours. Urgent orders prioritized. Check status in your orders list.

**Q: What if I need to modify an order after submitting?**
A: If order not yet approved, click "Edit" on order. If approved, contact pharmacist - they may be able to adjust.

**Q: Can I see previous deliveries for tracking historical data?**
A: Yes, click "Deliveries" → "Delivery History" to view all past deliveries for your patients.

**Q: How do I handle a medication that patient refuses?**
A: Document refusal in medication administration notes with reason. Message pharmacist. Don't force medication.

**Q: What's the difference between patient refusal and adverse reaction?**
A: Refusal = patient chooses not to take medication. Adverse reaction = patient has negative physical response to medication.

**Q: Can I delete or modify notes I've entered?**
A: You can edit notes for 24 hours. After that, contact facility admin. All changes are audited.

**Q: How do I handle narcotic/controlled substance orders?**
A: Same process, but requires facility-level approval. Your facility admin sets these restrictions.

**Q: What if the delivery address in the system is wrong?**
A: Contact patient directly to confirm correct address. Contact pharmacist before delivery to update address if needed.
